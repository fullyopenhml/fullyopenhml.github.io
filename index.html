Our reference design v0.2

The Fully Open Humanoid: Duke V2's joint layout on RobStride actuators, all-printed structure, BOM, printable STLs, URDF and a MuJoCo standing check. A design release, unbuilt, listed in the catalog on the same terms as everyone else.

Where our design comes from

Duke Humanoid V2 joint layout

General Robotics Lab, Duke. 31 RobStride joints, machined aluminium, 35 kg, about $13k in parts. We keep its leg topology (compact hip stack, short thigh, long shank), its bearing-supported joints, its XT30(2+2) daisy-chain harness and its parallel gripper.

Berkeley Humanoid Lite build philosophy

Hybrid Robotics, UC Berkeley. 22 DOF, all-printed, 16 kg, about $4.3k. We take its rule that every structural part comes off a desktop printer, its 2020-extrusion torso, its cheap mini PC and its habit of publishing filament in the BOM.

Build path

1 · Buy

Bill of materials

Every part, quantity and price, in tiers: core, optional kits, tools. Order the actuators first.

 2 · Print

Printing

34 printed parts as STL, 7 kg of filament, settings as numbers. Mount rings, couplers and plates; a 250 mm bed.

 3 · Assemble

Assembly

Three joint kits repeated 23 times, then legs, torso, arms, head, and the whole robot.

 4 · Wire

Electrical

48 V bus with a safety chain, four CAN daisy chains, every cable type and the actuator ID map.

 5 · Bring up

Bring-up

Bench supply first, IDs and zeros, then nine acceptance tests from bus integrity to a first step.

 6 · Run

Software

Isaac Lab and MuJoCo for training, a Python control loop on the robot, the URDF generated from this site's model.

Contribute

Everything here is generated from one file, tools/build_data.py: the 3D model, the BOM, the printed-parts list, the URDF and, through tools/build_parts.py, every STL. Change a dimension or a price there, run it, and every page updates. Open an issue or a pull request on GitHub. Licences and citation are on the reference page.
