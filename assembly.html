Assembly

Build bench subassemblies in this order, then join them. Every "parts needed" table below is counted from the 3D model; every step ends with a check you can do with your hands.

Order
  1. Set every actuator's CAN ID on the bench and label it.
  2. Build the 23 joint kits (three kinds).
  3. Pelvis, then two legs.
  4. Torso frame, electronics, head.
  5. Two arms, optional grippers.
  6. Final integration, then Electrical and Bring-up.
Read before lifting anything. Quasi-direct-drive actuators have no self-locking gears. A powered robot that loses power folds up immediately. From final integration on, the robot hangs from a hoist or a gantry rated for 50 kg, and the packs stay disconnected until the pre-power checks pass.

Before you start

  • All printed parts done, weighed and inserts set (printing page).
  • Every actuator has its CAN ID set on the bench and a label with joint, ID and bus (the ID plan). Do this now: the ID table is only editable in standby, and the wrong ID inside a closed limb costs an hour.
  • Tools: 2.5 / 3 / 4 mm hex drivers, a 1–10 N·m torque wrench, threadlocker, calipers, a soldering iron with insert tips, a deburring tool.
  • Screw torque: M3 1.2 N·m, M4 2.8 N·m, M5 5 N·m into heat-set inserts; M4 into the actuator flange 3.5 N·m with threadlocker. These are generic values for the screw sizes, not measured on this robot.

The three joint kits

Every joint is the same sandwich: the actuator body sits in a printed cup that belongs to the parent link; a printed output adapter bolts to the actuator's flange and belongs to the child link; a thin-section support bearing on the actuator's rear boss lets the child link also bear on the back of the actuator, so the adapter carries torque rather than bending. Build them as a batch.

1

Press the support bearing into its seat

The seat is in the cup or plate of the child link (for example the inner shank plate for the knee). Press square with a vice and a socket on the outer race only.

Check: the bearing sits flush, turns freely and cannot be pushed out by thumb.
2

Seat the actuator in its cup

Cable sockets facing the routing channel. Four M4 through the cup into the actuator's rear mounting holes, torqued in a cross pattern.

3

Bolt the output adapter to the flange

Threadlocker on every screw. Torque in a cross pattern in two passes. Read the flange pattern from the actuator manual; the adapter is designed to it.

Check: turned by hand, the adapter runs true with no wobble against a fixed pointer; no axial play; the actuator's rear boss enters the support bearing with a light push.

Pelvis

The pelvis shell carries the two hip-pitch actuators (outputs outboard) and the waist actuator (output up). Show on the model →

1

Fit the hip-pitch actuators

Slide each RS03 into the shell from the open side, output facing out, sockets facing up. Bolt through the shell's bottom and top plates.

Check: both flanges are coaxial: a 6 mm rod through both adapters' centre holes passes freely.
2

Fit the waist actuator

On top of the shell, output up, sockets toward the back. This joint's cup is the pelvis top plate itself.

Check: the waist adapter turns freely, level with the top plate.

Leg (build two)

Hip to foot: six joints, five of them RS03. Build the left leg from the table, then the right with mirrored prints and the right-side IDs. Show the left leg →

1

Hip-pitch adapter and hip-roll bracket

Bolt the hip-pitch adapter to the pelvis actuator's flange (kit step 3). Bolt the hip-roll bracket to the adapter with four M4. The bracket's cup faces backward. Model →

2

Hip-roll actuator

Seat the RS03 in the bracket cup, output forward, and its support bearing in the cup's rear disc.

Check: hip pitch turns through ±90° with the bracket clearing the pelvis shell by at least 3 mm.
3

Hip-yaw housing

Bolt the hip-roll adapter to the roll actuator's flange, then the hip-yaw housing to the adapter's boss. The housing's collar hangs below the roll actuator. Seat the hip-yaw RS03 in the collar, output down. Model →

Check: hip roll turns from −30° to +105° without the housing touching the pelvis; the yaw actuator's axis is vertical (spirit level on its rear face).
4

Thigh block and knee actuator

Bolt the thigh block to the hip-yaw adapter. Seat the knee RS03 in the block's cup, output outboard. This is Duke V2's short thigh: the knee actuator hangs directly under the yaw actuator. Model →

5

Shank

Bolt the outer shank plate (which carries the knee adapter) to the knee flange; fit the inner plate over the support bearing on the knee's rear boss; join the plates with the cross member. Seat the ankle-pitch RS03 between the plates at the bottom, output outboard through the outer plate.

Check: the knee turns 0° to 135° freely; the two plates are parallel within 0.5 mm (calipers top and bottom).
6

Ankle bracket and ankle-roll actuator

Bolt the ankle adapter to the ankle-pitch flange and the ankle bracket to it. Seat the RS06 in the bracket's rear cup, output forward, with its support bearing. Model →

7

Foot

Bolt the foot bracket to the ankle-roll flange, the foot plate to the bracket (four M5), the TPU sole to the plate.

Check: with every joint at zero the sole is level and the knee, ankle-pitch and hip-pitch axes are parallel (a straight edge along the outer plates touches all three adapters).
8

Route the harness

Six actuator drops from the hip-pitch actuator (in the pelvis) down to the ankle-roll actuator, then the terminator. Leave a loop at every joint sized for its full travel. See Routing.

Check: every joint moved to both limits by hand with no cable taut, pinched or rubbing.
9

The right leg

Repeat with mirrored prints and IDs 21–26 on can1. The shank cross member and the soles are symmetric and not mirrored.

Torso and head

Four 2020 uprights between two printed plates, electronics inside, shoulder-pitch actuators at the top corners, head on the top plate. Show on the model →

1

Frame

Bolt the four uprights to the bottom plate (M5 into the tapped ends), square them with the corner brackets, then the top plate. Square the frame before anything goes in: every limb references it.

Check: diagonals across the front face equal within 1 mm; the frame stands flat on a table without rocking.
2

Waist adapter

Bolt the waist adapter to the underside of the bottom plate. The torso will sit on the pelvis's waist actuator by this adapter.

3

Shoulder-pitch actuators

Seat one RS06 in each shoulder mount, output outboard, and bolt the mounts under the top plate. The flanges emerge through the side panels' clearance holes.

Check: both shoulder flanges are coaxial (rod test) and level with the top plate.
4

Electronics tray

Fix the tray to the front uprights with T-nuts. Mount the computer, the four CAN adapters (labelled with their bus), the USB hub, the distribution block and the 48→12 V converter. Mount the contactor and the breaker behind the tray; the E-stop and the breaker handle go through the back panel. Wiring is on Electrical.

5

IMU

Bolt the IMU rigidly to the bottom plate, axes aligned to the frame: X forward, Y left, Z up. The software applies no mounting rotation.

Check: the IMU cannot be moved by finger pressure; its silkscreen X arrow points at the front panel.
6

Battery cradles and packs

Two cradles on the bottom plate at the back; packs upright, strapped, leads toward the breaker. Leave both packs unplugged.

Check: a pack cannot shift when the frame is tilted 30° in any direction; each pack comes out without tools.
7

Head and panels

Neck post and head shell on the top plate. Side and back panels with M3 into inserts; the front panel with magnets, so the tray is reachable.

Arm (build two)

Shoulder to wrist: five joints. Show the left arm →

1

Shoulder adapter and roll bracket

Bolt the shoulder adapter to the torso's shoulder-pitch flange, then the shoulder-roll bracket to it, cup facing backward. Seat the shoulder-roll RS06 in the cup, output forward, with its support bearing. Model →

Check: shoulder pitch swings through 360° clearing the torso panels by 5 mm or more.
2

Upper-arm yoke and shoulder-yaw actuator

Roll adapter on the roll flange, yoke on the adapter boss. Seat the RS02 in the yoke's collar, output down.

Check: shoulder roll from −20° to +150° without the yoke touching the roll bracket.
3

Upper-arm link and elbow

Yaw adapter on the RS02 flange, upper-arm link on it. Seat the elbow RS02 in the link's cup, output outboard.

4

Forearm

Outer forearm plate (with the elbow adapter) on the elbow flange; inner plate on the support bearing; forearm body joins them and holds the wrist-roll RS05, output down.

Check: elbow 0° to 125° freely; the wrist axis is parallel to the upper-arm link within 1°.
5

Wrist adapter

Bolt the wrist adapter to the RS05 flange. The gripper, or any end effector, bolts to this adapter's four M3 inserts on a 30 mm square.

6

Route and repeat

Five drops from the shoulder-pitch actuator to the wrist, terminator at the wrist. The right arm uses mirrored prints and IDs 41–45 on can3.

Gripper (optional, build two)

Duke V2's rack-and-pinion parallel gripper on an STS3215 bus servo. Both servos share one Waveshare driver on the USB hub. Model →

1

Servo and pinion

Set the servo IDs (1 left, 2 right) on the driver first. Bolt the servo into the gripper body and the printed pinion onto its horn.

2

Racks, fingers and pads

Slide both racks in from opposite sides, both engaged on the pinion, jaws symmetric. Fit the fingers and glue the TPU pads.

Check: jaws move together, parallel, and meet flat at the centre.
3

Mount

Bolt the body to the wrist adapter with the jaws opening front-to-back at wrist zero. Run the servo cable up the arm with the CAN drops.

Final integration

Two people or a hoist from here on. Lift the torso by the top plate. Packs disconnected throughout.

1

Torso onto pelvis

Support the pelvis with both legs standing (or in a stand). Lower the torso so the waist adapter meets the waist flange; bolt it with the torso facing the same way as the feet.

Check: at waist zero, the top plate's front edge is parallel to the line of the two feet within 2 mm.
2

Arms

Bolt each arm's shoulder adapter to its torso flange. Check the side: the arms differ by IDs and bus.

3

Harness tails into the torso

Bring the four limb chains in through the bottom plate cut-outs and up to their CAN adapters. Waist actuator joins the left-arm chain (can2).

4

Inspect and hang

Every joint through its travel by hand, alone and together; no cable stretched, pinched or kinked in any pose; nothing rattles. Hang the robot from the top plate with the feet 10 cm off the floor.

Check: it hangs level, legs straight. Go to Pre-power checks.
