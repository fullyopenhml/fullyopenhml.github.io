The styled design: the K1 skeleton, built our way v2, work in progress

ROBOTIS' AI Sapiens K1 (open hardware, CERN-OHL-P-2.0) gives the skeleton: where every joint is, how long every limb is, the 23-joint layout, the 1.36 m size. This design builds that skeleton the way our production design is built: RobStride actuators in printed cups, a carbon-fibre tube wherever a link is long enough to have one, printed blocks where it is not, an extrusion torso, Amazing Hands. No covers yet: a 1X-style skin goes over it, so the structure only has to be right.

Structure: where the tubes are, and where they are not

At 1.36 m the limbs are short. A link gets a carbon-fibre tube only if, after the actuator cups at both ends, there is tube left to bond: the thigh and the shank qualify (); the upper arm is 115 mm from the shoulder-yaw output to the elbow and is a single printed block with the elbow cup in it; the forearm is a printed yoke with the wrist actuator on its far end, pointing forward as the K1's does. The torso is a 2020 frame 200 mm deep and 150 mm wide (the K1's is 205 × 220) between two laser-cut plates, with the waist actuator hanging under the bottom plate onto a printed waist plate across the pelvis bars. The hip stack is v1's: pitch in the pelvis cup, roll on the bracket, yaw hanging under the roll, with an RS03 everywhere.

The forearm-forward zero pose, and what it costs

The K1 hangs its hands beside the pelvis; ours cannot at the K1's arm line, because the RS03 hip stack is wider than the K1's. So the arm keeps the K1's convention: the elbow's zero is 90° of flexion, the forearm points forward, and the hands clear everything. Hanging the arm straight down is outside the range on purpose (the hand would meet the hip-roll actuator); the elbow goes 60° below horizontal, which hangs the hand 30° forward of vertical, clear of the pelvis bars, which were shortened to 240 mm for exactly that. With the forearm forward, an inward shoulder yaw brings the hand to the torso from 13°, so the yaw is limited inward; outward it is free. The torso-side clearance also moved the arm line 10 mm outboard and the shoulder-pitch actuators 13 mm further in than the K1's.

Torso packaging

The frame is 329 mm tall, the K1's shoulder height minus the waist stack, and the shoulder-pitch actuators sit inside it 17 mm apart, so the electronics live in three layers: the two 6S packs at the back and the computer at the front with the IMU under it, the CAN adapters and the USB hub above the computer, and the contactor, distribution block and buck converter above the shoulder actuators under the top plate. The E-stop and breaker are on the back panel. The electronics tray stops under the shoulder actuators.

Self-collision audit

The audit tests every part pair at the zero pose, in six preset poses (including the arms hanging), and through each joint's full range in 13 steps, with tubes declared inside their sockets and actuators inside their cups.

Does it stand? Physics check

The same MuJoCo check as v1: a floor, a floating base, one torque-limited position servo per joint, no balance controller. The servos hold the standing pose with the forearms hanging (elbows 60° below horizontal); the crouch ramps the legs and returns; the pushes are forward impulses on the torso. One setting differs from v1: the RS03 servos, which carry the hips and knees here, use the stiffness v1 gives its RS04 hips (320 N·m/rad, inside RobStride's position-mode range); at v1's RS03 setting the chain of three leg servos is softer than gravity in the crouch and the robot topples, which is a fact about position servos, not about the structure.

Why not keep the K1's shells as they are

Before this skeleton, the question was whether our actuators could simply go inside the K1's published shells. tools/build_k1rs.py measured the housing around every K1 joint from its meshes (the STLs contain the actuators, so the skin is the farthest ray hit) and found the trade: RobStride's 8:1 to 9:1 stages make the actuator wider than the K1's 20:1 DYNAMIXEL-Q at the same torque. Keeping the shells meant half the torque; keeping the torque meant growing the arm housings by 13 mm. The skeleton above resolves it by not keeping the shells: it keeps the joints and the size, takes the torque-first actuators, and leaves the body to a skin. The study's numbers are in k1rs.json.

What this needs next

  • The skin. The point of this skeleton is to disappear under a 1X-style soft cover. That is a pattern over the swept volumes of the links, with the joint ranges above as the constraint, and it is not started.
  • ROBOTIS' STEP files and BOM, announced as coming, would let the remaining deviations be compared against the K1's real parts rather than its meshes, and would show whether their ankle linkage is worth copying to bring our ankle down from 100 mm to their 64.
  • A walking policy. The URDF is ready for Isaac Lab; ROBOTIS' cyclo_lab environment for the K1 (Apache-2.0) is the natural starting point, with our actuator limits.
  • The push margin. Under position servos the robot takes 8 N·s and falls at 10, where the 60 N·m hip-pitch actuators saturate; v1 takes 10 with its 120 N·m RS04 hips. A standing policy would do better than a stiff servo; this check has no controller.
  • The joint-kit test that every design on this site depends on: print one RS03 cup, load it to 60 N·m.
