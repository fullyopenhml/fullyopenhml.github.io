The styled design, take two: AI Sapiens K1, cheaper inside work in progress

ROBOTIS published its AI Sapiens K1 humanoid as open hardware (CERN-OHL-P-2.0): the model, the meshes, the drawing, the controllers and the walking policy. So instead of drawing a body of our own, this design keeps the K1 exactly as published, the look, the proportions, the 23 joints, and swaps what makes it expensive: the DYNAMIXEL-Q actuators, which are not on sale yet, become RobStride actuators; the Jetson becomes our N100 and CAN electronics; the aluminium frame is printed where the loads allow; the rubber hands print in TPU from the published mesh.

Status: work in progress, second approach. The first attempt (printed cover shells over our v1 skeleton) was retired on 2026-10-02: a body drawn from primitives did not look like a product. This page takes the opposite route, a product's published body with our parts inside, and is honest about the one hard problem: a cheap actuator with the same torque does not fit the K1's housings, so every joint below shows what fits and what it costs in torque, or what torque needs and what it costs in millimetres. ROBOTIS' STEP files, bill of materials and printable covers are announced as coming; the parts of this page that depend on them are marked.

Why start from the K1

It is open hardware, not open-ish

Hardware under CERN-OHL-P-2.0 (permissive), software Apache-2.0, documentation CC BY 4.0. Published today: the URDF and STL meshes of every link, an MJCF model, the hardware drawing, ROS 2 bring-up, controllers and a Dynamixel hardware interface, and trained walking, squat and dance policies as ONNX files. Announced as coming: the hardware BOM, STEP files, 3D-printable covers and USD models.

It already looks the way we want

1355 mm tall, 292 mm across the shoulders, 205 mm deep, 35 kg, 23 joints (6 per leg, 5 per arm, a waist yaw), five-finger hands, a visor head with the depth camera behind it: the G1 class of proportions, with a rounded chest and tapered limbs. Every shell is in the meshes, so nothing has to be designed to look right.

Its cost is in parts we can swap

Twelve QC060 and eleven QC080 quasi-direct-drive actuators (48 V, RS-485, listed for Q1 2027 without a price), a Jetson Orin NX 16 GB, an aluminium frame, moulded covers. The announced robot price is in the mid-to-low 10 million KRW range, about $7,000 to $8,700. Actuators, computer, frame and covers are exactly the parts our v1 design already sources cheaply.

What we owe back

The derivative stays under CERN-OHL-P-2.0 with ROBOTIS credited as the source; our changes publish the same way. AI Sapiens and DYNAMIXEL are ROBOTIS' names, used here to say where the design comes from; ROBOTIS is not involved in this project.

Does a cheaper actuator fit? Joint by joint

The K1's STL meshes contain the actuators themselves, not just the skin: a ray cast from a joint axis first hits the actuator's Ø5 or Ø7 hollow shaft and last hits the cover. The last hit, taken in 48 directions at 17 stations along each axis, gives the inscribed radius of the housing around every joint; the table shows the largest. The fit rule is simpler and stricter than that measurement: a substitute fits without changing the shell if its body is no wider than the DYNAMIXEL-Q it replaces, because that is the envelope the housing was drawn around. Anything wider grows the housing by half the difference in diameter. RobStride's actuators have 7.75:1 to 9:1 planetary stages, the Q series 20:1, so at equal torque the RobStride is the wider part; that is the whole trade.

"Housing skin r" is the measured inscribed radius of the link that holds the actuator, at the best station within ±40 mm of the joint; it is a lower bound (flat faces and link ends pull it down), which is why the rule uses the K1 actuator's own diameter instead. Torque ratios are RobStride peak over DYNAMIXEL-Q peak; rated ratios are in the JSON.

Is half the torque enough?

The shape-first variant keeps every housing within 3.5 mm of the published shell and gives the legs 36 N·m peak where the K1 has 72.6, on a robot about 3 kg lighter. That is at the hip and knee. Our v0.2 budget design has 60 N·m on 28 kg (2.1 N·m per kg) and v1 has 120 N·m on 33 kg (3.6); the K1 itself has 2.1. So the shape-first robot sits at the low end of what walks: slow walking, no jumping, and a policy trained for exactly that. The torque-first variant restores 83 % of the K1's leg torque and 120 % of its arm torque, but grows the arm housings by 13 mm in radius, from Ø58 to Ø84.5 parts, and the leg housings by 10 mm; the arms would visibly change. The middle path that is probably right once the STEP files allow real CAD: torque-first at the hip pitch, hip yaw and knee, where the measured skin (r 45) hides most of an RS03's growth inside the tapered thigh, and shape-first everywhere else.

What this needs next

  • ROBOTIS' STEP files and BOM. Everything structural waits for them: the actuator cups get drawn at the K1's joint positions in CAD, the frame split between kept aluminium and printed brackets becomes real, the ankle push-rod geometry is read off rather than inferred from the drawing.
  • Printable covers. ROBOTIS lists them as coming; until then the shells are an estimate from the link meshes, which merge skin and internals and are not printable as they are.
  • Retrain the walking policy. cyclo_lab (Isaac Lab, Apache-2.0) and the K1 MJCF are published; the actuator model, torque limits and gains change to the RobStride's. The first useful experiment needs no hardware: run the published MJCF with torque clamped at 36 N·m and see what the published policy does.
  • An actuator with a 20:1 stage in RobStride's price class would remove the trade on this page. Candidates exist; none has been measured against the housings here, and none goes in the table without manual dimensions.
  • Hands. Print the published hand mesh in TPU and compare it with the Amazing Hand on the same wrist; the K1's hands are passive.
  • Then the usual: self-collision audit at the new housing sizes, the standing and push check, STL release, and the joint-kit test that everything on this site depends on.
