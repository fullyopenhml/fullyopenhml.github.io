Electrical

Two packs in series make a 44.4 V bus. It passes a breaker, a contactor held closed by the E-stop loop, and a distribution block, then splits into four limb chains. Each chain carries 48 V and CAN in one XT30(2+2) connector per actuator. 12 V for the computer comes off the same block.

Safety chain

  • Breaker: the main disconnect and pack over-current protection. Off whenever anyone works inside the torso.
  • Contactor: carries the motor bus; its 12 V coil is fed through the E-stop's normally-closed contact. Pressing the E-stop opens the coil circuit and drops the motor bus; the computer stays powered so logs survive.
  • E-stop on the back panel, a second one wired in series on a 3 m lead for the person on the hoist.
  • Fuses: 60 A on the pack lead inside the distribution block; 10 A on the 12 V feed.
  • TVS diodes across 48 V and ground at the distribution block and at each chain's end, to clamp regenerative spikes when the contactor opens under load.
  • A powered-down robot collapses. The E-stop is for stopping motion; the hoist is for not dropping the robot.

CAN bus

Four buses at 1 Mbit/s, one CANable PRO V2.0 each. Every bus is a single daisy chain, adapter → actuator → actuator, with a 120 Ω terminator at each physical end and no stubs. Six actuators per bus at a 500 Hz command rate is about 35 % bus load.

  • Leave a service loop at every joint sized for the joint's full travel, not one pose.
  • Sleeve limb runs in braided loom; tie to the printed brackets' loom slots, never across a moving edge.
  • Turn every joint to both ends by hand before a limb is closed: no cable taut, pinched or rubbing.

Pre-power checks

With no pack connected and the breaker off:

  1. Every 48 V conductor conducts end to end; + to − on each chain reads open (the drive capacitors charge slowly; no steady short).
  2. Every XT30(2+2): + on pin 1, − on pin 2, CAN_H on pin 4, checked against the pinout, not the colour.
  3. Each of the four buses reads about 60 Ω between CAN_H and CAN_L.
  4. The 48→12 V converter gives 12 V on a bench supply before it sees a pack.
  5. The E-stop loop: with 12 V applied, the contactor clicks in; pressing the E-stop drops it out.
  6. Both packs: no swelling, cells balanced within 0.02 V, then the series link.

Then First power-on, robot hanging.
