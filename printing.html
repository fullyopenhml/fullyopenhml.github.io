Printing

Every structural part is a plate, a cup or an adapter that prints flat on a 250 mm bed without supports on a load-bearing face. Three materials, three settings profiles, one printer.

Files. Every printed part is downloadable below as a binary STL in millimetres, generated by tools/build_parts.py from the same model as the viewer, with the actuator hole patterns, dowel holes, bearing pockets and locating recesses cut. Left-side files are published; parts marked mirror are flipped in the slicer for the right side. Nothing has been printed yet: check fits on the first part of each kind.

Orientation rules

  • Plates (shank, forearm, torso, foot) print flat on their largest face. Layer lines then run in the plane of the load, not across it.
  • Cups and adapters print with the bore vertical. The output-adapter discs print flange-face down so the bolted face is the smooth first layer.
  • Brackets with cups (hip-roll, hip-yaw, thigh, ankle, shoulder, yoke, upper arm) print on their plate face with the cup standing up; the mount rings are horizontal there and print without supports, the collars need supports only where they bridge to the plate. The print column gives the orientation per part.
  • Right-side parts marked mirror are the left STL mirrored in the slicer, not a separate file.

After printing

  • Bearing pockets are cut 0.2 mm over the bearing diameter, actuator bores 0.5 mm over the housing, coupler hubs 0.1 mm under the bearing bore. Printed holes shrink; ream or sand to fit: a bearing should press in by hand with a light tap, an actuator should slide in with no play.
  • Heat-set inserts: M3 in the wrist adapters and covers, M5 in the torso plates. The joint kits use through screws into the actuators, no inserts. Set inserts with a soldering iron at 230 °C, square, flush.
  • Clearance holes for M4 print at 4.4 mm; drill to 4.5 mm if the screw does not pass freely.
  • Weigh the first of each structural part and compare with the estimate in the table. A part 30 % heavier than the estimate has the wrong infill; 30 % lighter has under-extruded walls.
