Printing

Every structural part is a plate, a cup or an adapter that prints flat on a 250 mm bed without supports on a load-bearing face. Three materials, three settings profiles, one printer.

Files. v0.1 publishes the kinematic model (this site, the URDF). The printable STL, STEP and 3MF for each part are released with the v0.2 joint kits and onward; the table below gets a download column as each file lands, with a SHA-256. Until then the part list, masses and envelopes are what the design commits to.

Orientation rules

  • Plates (shank, forearm, torso, foot) print flat on their largest face. Layer lines then run in the plane of the load, not across it.
  • Cups and adapters print with the bore vertical. The output-adapter discs print flange-face down so the bolted face is the smooth first layer.
  • L-brackets (hip-roll, shoulder-roll, ankle) print on the plate face with the cup standing up; the cup's overhang is a 45° chamfer inside the model, so no supports.
  • Right-side parts marked mirror are the left STL mirrored in the slicer, not a separate file.

After printing

  • Bearing seats and actuator bores are modelled at nominal size. Ream or sand to fit: a bearing should press in by hand with a light tap, an actuator should slide in with no play.
  • Heat-set inserts: M4 in every actuator-flange adapter and cup, M5 in the torso plates, M3 in covers. Set them with a soldering iron at 230 °C, square, flush.
  • Clearance holes for M4 print at 4.4 mm; drill to 4.5 mm if the screw does not pass freely.
  • Weigh the first of each structural part and compare with the estimate in the table. A part 30 % heavier than the estimate has the wrong infill; 30 % lighter has under-extruded walls.
