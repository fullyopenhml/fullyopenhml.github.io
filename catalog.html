Open humanoids, catalogued

Every humanoid, biped and hand we could find that calls itself open, scored on the same nine questions, with the numbers the projects publish and the pages they were read from. Ours is in the list on the same terms as everyone else's.

How to read it. The score counts criteria met in full (1) or in part (½). "Open hardware" means a stated licence that lets anyone study, modify, make and sell the design; non-commercial terms and missing licences fail that test however good the files are. Costs are the projects' own figures unless marked. Nothing here has been re-measured by us.

What counts as open

The questions come from the Open Source Hardware Association's definition and from what it takes to actually build a robot from a release. A project is judged on what is public today, not on what is announced.

Open files around closed hardware

These publish URDFs, SDKs and training code and are useful to work with, but none gives a builder the mechanical source, electronics, BOM and permission needed to reproduce the robot. They are here so the checklist has its comparison row.

Add or correct a robot

The table is generated from tools/catalog_src.py, one Python dictionary per robot with a sources list. Open a pull request that edits the entry and cites where each changed fact was read; runs of python3 tools/build_catalog.py regenerate assets/data/catalog.json for this page and the builder. Corrections from the projects themselves are welcome above all.

Reference lists this catalog drew on: the Humanoids Data open-source audit, awesome-open-source-robots and the OSH Atlas.
